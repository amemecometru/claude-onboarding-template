## Access lifecycle

For each access request or cohort change, follow the client’s approved identity and change processes:

1. Confirm business approval, eligibility, and intended use.
2. Confirm identity/group membership and license availability with authorized administrators.
3. Apply access using the client’s approved process; record approval and change evidence.
4. Notify the user of scope, policy, onboarding, and support route.
5. Review access on the client-defined cadence and upon role change or departure; remove access as required by policy.
6. Reconcile assigned access and available reporting using authorized tools; investigate discrepancies.

Do not share administrator credentials or use another person’s account. Do not prescribe a specific role or admin setting without verifying current Microsoft documentation and the tenant’s role separation.

## Change and release handling

For a tenant configuration, policy, licensing, app, or user-experience change:

- Capture the requested change, business reason, impact, affected users, dependency, and risk.
- Identify the authorized approver and implementer; preserve separation of duties where client policy requires it.
- Check current Microsoft release information and tenant availability. A feature announcement does not guarantee availability in every tenant.
- Test in an approved scope where feasible; include accessibility and support impact.
- Communicate timing, user impact, action required, and help route.
- Record implementation, validation, issues, and rollback/mitigation options.
- Update user guidance and the source register if a documented behavior changed.

## Support and incident triage

Classify and route reports using client policy. Examples include access or app failure, inaccurate output, inaccessible content, unexpected data visibility, suspected policy violation, and possible sensitive-data exposure. For a potential security/privacy incident, preserve evidence only through approved procedures and escalate promptly; do not ask users to paste sensitive output into general support tickets.

For each issue, record only necessary information in the approved system: timestamp, category, affected service/scenario, severity, owner, actions, resolution, and whether user guidance or a control needs review.

## Recurring service review

At the cadence approved by the service owner, review:

- License/access assignments and user lifecycle reconciliation.
- Current approved groups and use cases; remove obsolete scope.
- Content access issues raised by content owners and status of remediation.
- Support trends, training gaps, accessibility feedback, and champion capacity.
- Pilot/production measures alongside their definitions and limitations.
- Incidents, control exceptions, changes, and open risk decisions.
- Current Microsoft product documentation, release changes, licensing, and tenant availability.
- Whether current policy, source materials, and end-user instructions remain accurate.

Record decisions, owners, due dates, and escalation. Do not report “compliant,” “secure,” “productive,” or “ROI achieved” without evidence and the appropriate client owner’s approval.
