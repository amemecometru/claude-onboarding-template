## B. Define measures before starting

Select a small set of measures tied to the intended workflow. For every measure, document a definition, source, owner, collection frequency, privacy boundary, and known limitation.

| Measure type | Example measure | Definition to agree | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach / readiness | Share of invited cohort completing orientation | Completion numerator / eligible denominator | Completion does not show proficiency or business value. |
| Engagement | Active use for an approved scenario | Define activity and period using an approved source | Usage can be misread; telemetry availability and meaning vary. |
| Task outcome | Time to complete a task | Start/stop rules, task complexity, sample | Self-report and task difficulty can bias comparison. |
| Quality | Reviewer-rated correctness/completeness | Rubric, reviewer, sample, and source of truth | A small sample is not a general accuracy guarantee. |
| Rework | Corrections or follow-up effort | Define what counts and how recorded | More careful review may initially increase recorded effort. |
| User experience | Confidence, usefulness, friction | Same questions at baseline and follow-up | Sentiment is not objective productivity evidence. |
| Risk / safety | Reported issues, near misses, policy exceptions | Severity categories and reporting route | Zero reports does not prove zero risk. |
| Support | Tickets / help requests by theme | Categorization and time window | Changes in reporting habits affect counts. |
| Cost / value | License and operating cost versus agreed benefit | Approved cost and benefit method | Avoid unsupported ROI or causal claims. |

Do not collect personal or sensitive information unnecessarily. Get the client’s approval for telemetry, survey/interview, retention, access, and aggregation. If using a Microsoft dashboard or admin report, verify availability, data definitions, licensing, permissions, and privacy implications in the tenant and current documentation.

## C. Baseline questionnaire

Use the same wording before and after the pilot where possible. Let respondents skip questions and avoid collecting names unless approved.

1. How often do you perform [scenario]?
2. About how long does a typical task take, including review and corrections? [Range / not sure]
3. What steps or handoffs take the most effort?
4. What tools and approved sources do you use today?
5. What errors, rework, or delays commonly occur?
6. How confident are you completing the task? [Consistent scale]
7. What would make this task safer or easier?
8. What information or actions must not be used in the pilot?

## G. Reporting language

Use evidence-calibrated statements. Examples:

- **Good:** “In this 12-person, six-week pilot, 8 participants reported using the approved scenario weekly. Task-time estimates were self-reported and were not independently observed.”
- **Avoid:** “Copilot increased productivity by 40%” unless the method supports that causal and quantitative claim.
- **Good:** “Three participants identified unclear instructions; the guide was revised and retested with two participants.”
- **Avoid:** “Users validated the enterprise solution” when only a small usability review occurred.

Record cohort, dates, method, denominator, missing data, limitations, and permission before sharing results outside the client.
