# Session Runbook — the 60–90 minute onboarding session

The session is the product. The install is just setup. Run it on the team's
real work, not a demo.

## Before the first visit

Ask these questions first — in plain words, on a call or in person. Write
the answers down; the whole setup is built from them.

1. What does the business do, and what does the team spend its day on?
   Write down the three most repeated jobs.
2. What AI tools do they already pay for, who uses them, and who does not?
   Never sell a second subscription for a job the first one already covers.
3. Who is on the team? Who is comfortable with new software, and who is
   not? The onboarding is built around the least comfortable person.
4. What do they want Claude to do for them — and what must it absolutely
   never touch (client data, money, anything regulated)? Write both lists
   down and keep them in front of you during the setup.
5. Who is the single point of contact on their side? That person answers
   questions when you are gone and owns the passwords.

Then set up before the session: Claude Desktop (and Claude Code for anyone
who writes code) installed under each person's own work email, one shared
place Claude can read, the three repeated jobs written as skills. See
`skills/claude-team-onboarding/SKILL.md` for the full setup steps.

## The session (60–90 minutes, whole team present)

**First 15 minutes — show one real job, start to finish.**
Pick the simplest of the three repeated jobs and do it on their own work,
in front of them. Not a demo, not a slide. One real job, done.

**Next 30 minutes — each person does one real task.**
They drive, you watch. Fix mistakes as they happen. Do not do it for them.
If someone gets stuck, that stuck point is the most valuable thing in the
session — write it down.

**Next 15 minutes — questions.**
Write every question down, even the ones that sound basic. The basic ones
are the ones that block people later. If a question shows a gap in a skill,
note it to fix that day.

**Last 15 minutes — agree on the week.**
Each person names the one job they will try Claude on this week, and the
team agrees on the one job nobody will let it near. Write both lists down
and leave them with the point of contact.

Leave behind the one-page sheet (`leave-behind.md`): how to open each tool,
where the skills live, who the point of contact is, and the rule that
nothing gets sent without a human reading it first.

## Afterward

- **One week later:** one 30 minute follow up. Ask what worked, what did
  not, and what surprised them. Keep a shared questions document — every
  question goes in with its answer, so nobody asks the same thing twice.
- **When a skill has a gap:** fix the skill that day and tell the team what
  changed.
- **When the tool cannot do something:** say so plainly. Never promise a
  feature the tool does not have.
- **After a month:** check in once more. Which of the three jobs is actually
  faster now, and which one is not? Drop the skill for the one that is not,
  and write a skill for whatever replaced it.
